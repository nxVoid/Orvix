# First collection art direction

## Shared surfaces

Use the inherited Relay typeface. Neutral ink #0D0D12, raised #16161E and card #1B1B25 establish the studio canvas. Primary text #F2F0F7 and secondary #A5A2B3 preserve readability. #9061F9 is reserved for selected navigation, primary actions and small emphasis. #B79AFF supports focus and premium metadata. Borders use neutral low-contrast strokes.

## Quantum Horizon

A wide cinematic alien landscape at blue hour. Multiple naturally fractured mountain layers retreat into violet-grey haze. Dark obsidian foreground has sparse mineral texture. A small, far-away energy event lights a narrow section of horizon on the right. The left lower third is quiet for the avatar overlap and the center-left sky stays clear for UI cropping. No grid, floating frame, star scatter or logo.

Motion is a 24-second rise-and-return of thin distant haze, with a maximum six-pixel displacement at the 960-pixel master. Light intensity varies subtly around the same localized horizon event. The landscape stays stable. Return endpoints and slopes match. Reduced motion uses the poster. Cards use static artwork. Expanded visible previews may animate.

## Violet Halo

Fine satin titanium arcs with violet light confined to the inner bevel. Asymmetric recessed joins create a manufactured material. A quiet highlight travels along a partial arc over 12 seconds without pulsing the complete rim. The aperture inside radius 93 of a 256-pixel square remains transparent. Poster and 32-pixel representation retain a single clear silhouette.

## Amethyst Crown

Dark brushed metal base supports five faceted amethyst crystals above the avatar. Faces have independent directional shading and small pale edge reflections. Side crystals taper into the ring. Purple occupies the mineral, with quiet metal elsewhere. A very low-amplitude 18-second specular pass touches only the crown tips. No global rotation or flashing. Face aperture remains transparent.

## Midnight Orbit

A near-black orbital support with three restrained satellite markers at distinct depths. Rear orbit uses a dim thin ellipse. Front arc has a small pale lavender rim. Satellites follow a 20-second ellipse with one stable orbit inclination. Orbit stays outside the protected face circle, with a static fallback in the same composition.
